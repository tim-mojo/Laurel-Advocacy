How Emergency Shelter Changed One Family's Story

RC Renee Castillo · Aug 14, 2026 · 6 min read
[IMAGE 16:9 — Maria and her children outside their new apartment]

When Maria arrived at our emergency shelter on a Tuesday night in February, she had her two children, one duffel bag, and nowhere else to go. She and her kids had been staying in her car for four nights after an unsafe living situation left them with no other option. "I didn't know shelters like this existed," she told us later. "I thought we were completely alone."

She wasn't. Within an hour of her intake call to our 24/7 crisis line, an advocate had a bed ready for her family, along with a hot meal and a safety plan. That first night is the moment our Emergency Services team exists for — and it's only the beginning of the story.

The first thirty days

Maria's case manager met with her the next morning to talk through options, not just for that night, but for the weeks ahead. Emergency shelter gave her family a safe place to sleep while the rest of a plan came together: enrolling her children in school, connecting her with a legal aid partner, and beginning the paperwork for our Housing Services program.

"Nobody rushed me," Maria said. "They asked what I needed, and then they actually listened."

"They asked what I needed, and then they actually listened."

Moving into transitional housing

After five weeks in emergency shelter, Maria and her children moved into one of our transitional housing units — a two-bedroom apartment where she could begin rebuilding without the pressure of full market rent. Her housing navigator worked alongside her to build a budget, and her case manager connected her with childcare so she could attend job interviews.

Building toward independence

Maria enrolled in our Self-Sufficiency Program the same month she moved into transitional housing. Over twelve weeks, she completed job readiness training, sat down with a financial coach to build her first budget in years, and interviewed for three positions before accepting a job as a medical office assistant.

  • Completed job readiness training and resume coaching
  • Built an emergency savings fund with financial coaching support
  • Secured stable employment within four months of intake

A home of her own

Six months after that first night in emergency shelter, Maria signed a one-year lease on a two-bedroom apartment of her own. Her housing navigator helped her negotiate the deposit, and our Development team connected her with a local partner who furnished the apartment's first bedroom.

"I used to think asking for help meant I'd failed," Maria said. "Now I know it's how we got here." Her family is one of more than 1,200 we serve each year — and their story is a reminder of why Laurel Advocacy's work matters, one family at a time.
