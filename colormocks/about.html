About Laurel Advocacy

For 45 years, we've stood beside families and individuals in our community facing crisis — offering shelter, services, and a path toward self-sufficiency.

Memberships

Together, we create lasting change

Our Mission

Laurel Advocacy exists to support neighbors in crisis with emergency services, safe housing, and the tools for lasting self-sufficiency — delivered with dignity, and free of judgment.

Our Vision

We envision a community where every family has a safe place to sleep, a path out of crisis, and the ongoing support needed to thrive on their own terms.

Our History.

  1. 1987.

    LARS is founded by a group of local congregation leaders and community members who seek a joint approach to helping those who came to their doors for food and financial help.

    Establishes the Emergency Services Program (ES) to provide groceries, financial support and homeless services and is housed by St. Phillip's Episcopal Church for the first two years.

  2. 1999.

    A building is purchased at 311 Laurel Avenue, which has been LARS' headquarters ever since.

  3. 2004.

    The Turkey Trot, LARS' flagship event for fundraising and community awareness, has its inaugural run Thanksgiving morning, and continues to this day.

  4. 2010.

    Headquarters at 311 Laurel Avenue undergoes much-needed renovation with the addition of a second floor.

    LARS' Permanent Supportive Housing Program (PSH) launches to provide housing to chronically homeless and disabled individuals.

  5. 2017.

    LARS' Self-Sufficiency Program (SSP) launches to provide motivated clients with much-needed life skills, workforce development, and coaching for economic stability.

  6. 2020.

    During the COVID-19 pandemic, LARS operates at full capacity, experiencing surges in requests for assistance that grow three-fold in the next 4 years.

  7. 2022.

    Due to the team's exemplary work, LARS' PSH Program is ranked as the #1 housing program in Prince George's County.

    LARS expands its food distribution schedule from three to five days a week.

  8. 2024.

    Second LARS property is purchased at 309 Laurel Avenue.

    LARS receives a HUD expansion grant that more than doubles the program's budget from $650,000 to over $1.5M.

    LARS launches two new programs to meet the growing needs of the community:

    1) THRIVE — Targeted Help, Resources and Inclusion for Vulnerable Elders, providing food and supportive services to seniors.

    2) Project GROW — Growing Resources, Opportunity & Well-being, teaching clients to raise vegetables in small containers and balcony gardens.

  9. 2025.

    HOME: Hotel Outreach for Mobilization & Empowerment launches, providing food, financial, and housing supportive services to individuals and families living in local motels.
